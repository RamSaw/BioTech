## Concepts in play (should be able to explain each without notes)

- **Why the geometric mean** for aggregating binding affinities: affinities are log-normally
  distributed, so the arithmetic mean is dominated by weak binders with large nM values.
- **Why 500 nM** as the positive threshold — and that it is a convention, not a physical constant.
- **Why negatives were synthesised at all**: the IEDB export is ~91% positive, and its real
  negatives are 95% 9-mers, so training on them teaches "length > 9 → binder".
- **Why lengths were sampled from the positive distribution**: to deny the model that length
  shortcut.

## Verdict on the previous result

Not trustworthy yet, through no fault of the modelling. Two problems, both in the evaluation rather
than the model:

1. Negatives are random sequences, so the task is partly "real peptide vs random string" rather than
   "binder vs non-binder". Composition alone may carry most of the score.
1. The split is random over near-duplicate peptides, so the test set partly measures memorisation.

Neither is visible from the metrics table, which is exactly why this is the interesting part.
